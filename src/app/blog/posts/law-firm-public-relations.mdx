Every channel a firm can buy has the same flaw: the prospect knows you paid for it. A billboard, a search ad, and a sponsored listing all say the same thing, which is that the firm has a budget. **Law firm public relations** is the one marketing discipline where somebody else makes the claim for you, and that is precisely why it moves people that advertising does not.

Most firms get it wrong in one of two ways. They either ignore it entirely, or they pay a retainer for a stream of press releases about awards and new hires that no journalist will ever open. Neither approach produces a signed case.

## What Law Firm Public Relations Actually Does

Law firm public relations is not a lead channel. Treat it as one and you will cancel it in ninety days.

What it produces is third-party validation that makes every other channel convert better. A prospect who clicks your ad and then searches your name will find either a website and a directory profile, or a website, a directory profile, and a local news story quoting your partner on a case like theirs. Those are different firms in the prospect's mind.

Earned coverage does four distinct jobs:

- **It changes what a name search returns.** News results sit beside your own pages when someone vets you, and they carry weight your own copy cannot. This is the same mechanism behind the [trust signals](/blog/law-firm-trust-signals) that get a case signed before the consultation.
- **It builds authority that search engines and AI systems read.** Links and mentions from news organizations are among the hardest signals to manufacture, which is why they matter for [law firm SEO strategy](/blog/law-firm-seo-strategy) and for being cited in AI-generated answers.
- **It drives referrals from other lawyers.** Attorneys send cases to the name they have seen attached to a result. Referring lawyers read trade press far more than consumers do.
- **It differentiates.** In a market where every firm claims to fight for you, being the lawyer a reporter calls for comment is a position competitors cannot simply buy.

None of these shows up as a tracked call the week the story runs. That is the central management problem with PR, and we will come back to it.

## What Actually Earns Coverage

Reporters do not cover law firms. They cover stories, and occasionally a law firm is useful to one.

The firm that understands this stops pitching itself and starts pitching things a newsroom can use. In practice, earned media for law firms comes from a short list of sources.

### Expert commentary

This is the most repeatable form of coverage and the one most firms underuse. A local reporter covering a trucking crash, a new state law, or a recall needs someone who can explain what it means in two plain sentences, today, before deadline.

The lawyer who responds in twenty minutes with a clean, quotable answer gets called again. The one who responds tomorrow with four paragraphs of caveats does not.

### Cases with public significance

A filing or verdict is newsworthy when it affects people beyond the parties: a defective product still on shelves, a pattern of conduct by a local employer, a dangerous intersection the city ignored. A large number alone is weaker than firms assume, and a confidential settlement is not a story at all.

### Original data

Firms sit on information reporters want. Intake records can show which intersections generate the most crash calls, how long local insurers take to respond, or how wage complaints shift by industry. Aggregated and anonymized, this becomes a story only your firm can supply.

### Community and access work

Free clinics, pro bono matters, and scholarship programs earn modest local coverage. It rarely drives cases directly, but it populates the name-search results with something other than advertising.

What does not earn coverage is equally consistent: "best lawyer" badges, office moves, routine hires, and anniversary announcements. A law firm press release on any of these is a document written for the partners, not for the press.

## The Ethics Rules That Govern Attorney Media Relations

A consumer brand can say nearly anything to a reporter. A lawyer cannot, and that is what separates law firm public relations from corporate PR. The constraints are specific.

### Trial publicity

[ABA Model Rule 3.6](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/professional_responsibility/publications/model_rules_of_professional_conduct/rule_3_6_trial_publicity/) prohibits a lawyer participating in a matter from making public statements they know or reasonably should know will have a substantial likelihood of materially prejudicing the proceeding. The rule also lists what is generally safe to state, including the claim or defense involved, information in a public record, and the scheduling or result of any step in litigation.

In practice, that means quoting the complaint is safer than characterizing the defendant, and commenting on evidence that may be inadmissible is where lawyers get into trouble. State versions of these trial publicity rules vary, so check your jurisdiction's text before a spokesperson goes on camera.

### Confidentiality

[Model Rule 1.6](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/professional_responsibility/publications/model_rules_of_professional_conduct/rule_1_6_confidentiality_of_information/) covers information relating to the representation, not just privileged communications. The fact that something appears in a court file does not automatically free you to publicize it.

Get informed consent from the client, in writing, before any media outreach about their matter. Make sure they understand that coverage is permanent and that they cannot control the comment section.

### Truthful communication

[Model Rule 7.1](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/professional_responsibility/publications/model_rules_of_professional_conduct/rule_7_1_communications_concerning_a_lawyer_s_services/) bars false or misleading communications about a lawyer's services, and it applies to what you tell a reporter and what you put in a release, not only to paid ads. Announcing a verdict without context that it is under appeal, or implying that a past result predicts a future one, creates the same exposure as it would in an advertisement. The broader framework is covered in our guide to [attorney advertising compliance](/blog/attorney-advertising-compliance).

One practical safeguard covers most of this: no statement about an active matter leaves the firm without sign-off from the responsible attorney, regardless of who drafted it.

## Building a Legal PR Strategy Without a Big-Firm Budget

A workable program for a small or midsize firm is mostly discipline, not spend.

1. **Pick two or three topics you can own.** A firm that comments on everything is the expert on nothing. Choose subjects tied to the cases you want, such as trucking safety, nursing home staffing, or wage theft in a specific industry.
2. **Build a list of twenty journalists, not two thousand.** Identify the local and trade reporters who cover those topics. Read their work. A targeted note to the right reporter outperforms a wire distribution to everyone.
3. **Designate one spokesperson and train them.** Media relations rewards consistency. Train that person to give short, plain answers and to decline a question without sounding evasive.
4. **Set a response standard.** Reporter inquiries get an answer within the hour during business hours. Treat it with the same urgency as a new lead, because the window closes just as fast.
5. **Create a reason to call you.** Publish one data-driven piece per quarter from your own intake or case records. Send it to your list with a two-line summary of the finding.
6. **Reuse every placement.** Coverage that runs once and disappears is half wasted. Add it to practice pages, intake follow-up emails, and consultation materials.

### In-house or agency

An agency brings existing reporter relationships and pitching skill, usually on a monthly retainer with a multi-month minimum. That makes sense when the firm has a steady flow of newsworthy cases or is entering a new market.

For most firms, the better starting point is internal: a marketing lead who owns the reporter list and a partner willing to pick up the phone. If six months of that produces nothing, the constraint is the story supply, and an agency will not fix it.

## Measuring Law Firm Public Relations Without Vanity Metrics

PR reports are notorious for numbers that sound large and mean little. "Potential reach" in the millions and "advertising value equivalency" tell you nothing about whether the firm signed more cases.

Measure what connects to the business instead:

- **Branded search volume.** If more people search your firm name after sustained coverage, PR is working. Google Search Console shows this for free.
- **Referring domains from news sources.** Count the links and mentions from real publications, and watch whether rankings on your priority pages move.
- **Attorney referrals.** Ask every referring lawyer how they came to know the firm, and log the answer.
- **Intake source answers.** Add "saw you in the news" as an option when intake asks how the caller heard of you. Self-reported data is imperfect, but it captures what click tracking never will.
- **Conversion rate on other channels.** Compare signed-case rates on paid leads before and after a period of strong coverage. A lift there is PR's real return.

No single-touch model will credit a news story for a case that arrived through a search ad three weeks later. That is a limitation of the model, not evidence the story did nothing, which is the core argument for multi-touch [marketing attribution](/blog/law-firm-marketing-attribution).

Give the effort two to three quarters before judging it. Reputation compounds slowly, and then it becomes very hard for a competitor to replicate.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is law firm public relations?

Law firm public relations is the practice of earning unpaid coverage and third-party credibility for a firm through news media, trade publications, and expert commentary. Unlike advertising, the firm does not control the final message, which is what makes the result more persuasive. Its main effect is to raise conversion on every other channel rather than to generate leads directly.

### How much does law firm public relations cost?

Costs range from almost nothing for an internally run program to a monthly agency retainer that typically runs in the thousands of dollars with a minimum term. The real cost of an in-house effort is attorney time for interviews and fast responses to reporters. Start internally, and hire an agency only when you have more newsworthy material than you can pitch yourself.

### Can a lawyer talk to the media about an active case?

Yes, within limits. Under trial publicity rules such as ABA Model Rule 3.6, a lawyer may generally state the claims involved and information in the public record, but may not make statements likely to materially prejudice the proceeding. Client consent is also required before publicizing information about the representation.

### Is a law firm press release still worth sending?

A law firm press release is worth sending only when it contains actual news, such as a significant filing, a verdict with public impact, or original data. Releases about awards, hires, and anniversaries are almost never picked up. A direct, personalized pitch to a reporter who covers the topic usually outperforms a mass release.

### How long does a legal PR strategy take to show results?

A legal PR strategy typically takes two to three quarters to show measurable results. Early signs are rising branded search volume and repeat calls from the same reporters. Signed-case impact follows later, usually as higher conversion on paid and referral channels.

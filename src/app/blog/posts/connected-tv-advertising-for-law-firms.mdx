Most firms still treat television as a brand-awareness line item they cannot afford or cannot track. Connected TV advertising for law firms changes the first half of that sentence and, if you set it up correctly, the second half too.

Connected TV (CTV) means ads served through streaming apps and smart TVs rather than a cable or broadcast schedule. You buy impressions by household, geography, and audience, not by a 30-second slot at 6 p.m. The catch is that CTV is easy to buy badly, and the firms that treat it like a cheaper billboard usually conclude it does not work.

## What Connected TV Advertising for Law Firms Actually Is

CTV ads run inside ad-supported streaming services and on platforms that stream through a smart TV, a streaming stick, or a game console. The viewer cannot skip most of them, and they play full screen on the largest display in the house.

That combination explains why legal advertisers are paying attention. Traditional TV sells you a market-wide audience, most of whom will never need a lawyer in your practice area. CTV lets you narrow delivery to zip codes, DMAs, or household-level segments.

Three formats matter for law firms:

- **Standard CTV video:** 15 or 30-second spots inside streaming content, bought programmatically.
- **Streaming audio and video bundles:** Cross-device packages that pair a TV spot with a mobile or desktop follow-up.
- **Direct publisher buys:** Placements with a specific network or streaming service, usually at higher minimums.

For most firms under 50 attorneys, programmatic buying through a demand-side platform is the practical starting point.

## Why Firms Are Moving Budget From Broadcast to CTV

Linear television has two problems for a regional firm: waste and rigidity. You pay to reach viewers in counties you do not serve, and you commit to a flight schedule weeks in advance.

CTV flips both. You can launch a campaign in one metro, pause it, and reallocate within days. Minimums are lower, so a single-office firm can test with a budget that would not buy a week of local broadcast.

The [Clio Legal Trends Report](https://www.clio.com/resources/legal-trends/) has consistently shown that prospective clients shop around and decide quickly, which makes top-of-mind awareness before the legal problem arrives more valuable. CTV is one of the few channels that builds that awareness at a household level without the cost structure of broadcast.

There is a tradeoff. Unlike search, CTV does not capture someone who already has a problem. It creates familiarity so that when they search, your name is one they recognize. For context on what that search stage costs, read our breakdown of [legal advertising costs](/blog/legal-advertising-costs).

## Where CTV Fits in a Law Firm Marketing Budget

CTV is an upper-funnel channel. It should not replace paid search or referrals, and it will not rescue a firm with a weak intake process.

A reasonable starting allocation for a firm already spending on search and local SEO is **5 to 10 percent of total ad spend**, held for at least 90 days before judging results. Pulling the plug at 30 days is the most common mistake, because awareness effects show up in branded search and call volume with a lag.

If you are still setting your overall spend, our guide to the [law firm marketing budget](/blog/law-firm-marketing-budget) walks through how to size channels before adding a new one.

### Which practice areas fit CTV best

CTV rewards practice areas where the client has time to remember you and where the case value justifies a longer path to signing:

1. Personal injury and wrongful death, where lifetime value is high and category competition is crowded.
2. Estate planning and elder law, where viewers skew older and watch more streaming TV.
3. Family law and immigration, where geographic and language targeting matter.
4. Workers' compensation and Social Security disability in high-density markets.

Emergency-driven practices such as criminal defense rely more on search at the moment of arrest, so CTV plays a supporting role at best.

## Targeting for Connected TV Advertising for Law Firms

Targeting is where CTV earns its premium over broadcast. The common options, from most to least reliable for a law firm:

- **Geographic:** Zip code, county, or DMA. Always the foundation.
- **Contextual:** Content categories such as local news, crime dramas, or financial programming that correlate with your audience.
- **Demographic and household:** Age bands, homeownership, or income tiers supplied by the platform or a data partner.
- **Retargeting:** Serving a CTV ad to households that visited your website. This links the channel to your [retargeting ads](/blog/retargeting-ads-for-law-firms) program.

Be careful with behavioral segments that claim to identify people who were in an accident or filing for divorce. Vendors rarely document how those segments are built, and you inherit the compliance risk. Ask for the source and consent basis before you pay for any audience.

## Creative That Works on a Living Room Screen

A CTV spot is not a repurposed billboard or a talking-head from your homepage. Viewers are leaning back, and the ad runs between shows they chose.

What tends to perform:

- Open with the viewer's situation, not your firm name, in the first five seconds.
- Use one attorney, on camera, speaking plainly. Production polish matters less than credibility.
- Keep a single call to action: one phone number or one short URL, shown for the whole last third.
- Build a dedicated [landing page](/blog/law-firm-landing-pages) so the person who types your URL after a TV ad does not land on a generic homepage.

Because CTV viewers rarely click, the URL or QR code needs to be simple enough to remember or scan from a couch.

### Compliance on the screen

Every claim in a TV spot is subject to the same rules as any other lawyer advertising. [Model Rule 7.1 from the American Bar Association](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/professional_responsibility/publications/model_rules_of_professional_conduct/rule_7_1_communications_concerning_a_lawyer_s_services/) prohibits false or misleading communications about a lawyer's services, and states layer their own disclaimer, testimonial, and dramatization requirements on top.

Review your state's rules on on-screen disclaimers and any restrictions on results claims before production, not after. Our overview of [attorney advertising compliance](/blog/attorney-advertising-compliance) covers the common traps.

## Measuring CTV Without a Click

This is the part that makes or breaks the channel. CTV rarely produces a direct, trackable click, so judging it by last-click attribution guarantees it looks like a failure.

Use a layered approach instead:

1. **Dedicated tracking number and vanity URL** used only in the CTV creative.
2. **Geo-based lift testing:** Run CTV in one market and hold out a comparable one. Compare branded search volume and call volume across both.
3. **Post-intake source questions:** Train intake to ask "How did you hear about us?" and record answers in your CRM.
4. **Branded search trend:** Watch for a sustained rise in searches for your firm name during and after flights.

None of these is perfect on its own. Together they give a defensible read. For the broader framework, see our guide to [law firm marketing attribution](/blog/law-firm-marketing-attribution).

Insight from the [Thomson Reuters Institute](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en/institute.html) on client acquisition and firm economics reinforces a simple point: firms that cannot connect spend to signed matters tend to cut the channels that quietly feed the top of the funnel.

## A 90-Day CTV Test Plan

Keep the first test small and structured.

- **Weeks 1 to 2:** Produce one 30-second spot and a 15-second cutdown. Set up the tracking number, vanity URL, and landing page.
- **Weeks 3 to 10:** Run in one metro with zip-level targeting. Hold frequency at roughly 3 to 5 impressions per household per week.
- **Weeks 11 to 13:** Compare branded search, call volume, and signed cases against the holdout market and the prior period.

Decide in advance what result justifies expansion. Without a threshold, every outcome looks like a success to someone.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does connected TV advertising for law firms cost?

Connected TV advertising for law firms typically runs on a CPM basis, meaning you pay per thousand impressions, and rates vary widely by market and targeting. Many programmatic platforms accept monthly budgets in the low-to-mid four figures for a single metro test. Expect higher CPMs for narrow audience segments and premium streaming inventory.

### Does CTV advertising generate cases for personal injury firms?

CTV generates cases indirectly by building familiarity that converts through search, referrals, and direct calls. Personal injury firms in competitive metros often see the effect in branded search and call volume rather than in clicks. Judge it over at least 90 days using a dedicated tracking number and a holdout market.

### Is CTV better than traditional TV for a local law firm?

CTV is usually the better starting point for a local firm because it allows zip-code targeting, lower minimums, and faster adjustments. Traditional TV still offers broader mass reach and can suit firms with a statewide footprint. The right choice depends on your service area and budget.

### How do you track results from streaming TV ads?

You track streaming TV ads through dedicated phone numbers and URLs, geographic lift tests, intake source questions, and branded search trends. Last-click attribution undercounts CTV, so no single metric is enough. Combine several signals and compare against a market where you did not run the campaign.

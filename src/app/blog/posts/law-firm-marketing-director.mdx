Most firms hire a **law firm marketing director** for the wrong reason. A managing partner gets tired of juggling an agency, a website vendor, and a stack of lead sources, so they hand the whole mess to one person and hope. Six months later the title has changed but the cost per signed case has not.

The role works when it is built around accountability for signed cases, not activity. This guide covers when the hire makes sense, what the job should own, and how to tell whether it is working.

## When a Law Firm Marketing Director Makes Sense

Marketing spend is the trigger, not firm headcount. A firm spending under roughly $10,000 a month on marketing rarely generates enough decisions to keep a full-time director busy, and an agency or fractional lead is usually the better fit.

The signals that the hire is overdue:

- Monthly marketing spend has passed $25,000 to $30,000 across multiple channels
- Nobody can say which channel produced last quarter's signed cases
- The managing partner still approves ad copy and reviews landing pages
- Agency reports describe clicks and impressions rather than retained clients
- Intake and marketing blame each other when lead volume and signed cases diverge

If three or more apply, the firm has outgrown informal coordination. Our breakdown of [how much a firm should spend on marketing](/blog/law-firm-marketing-budget) helps size the budget the role will manage.

## What a Law Firm Marketing Director Should Actually Own

The title is vague, so the job description has to be specific. A director who owns only brand and content will not move case volume.

### Budget and channel allocation

The director decides where dollars go and when to cut them. That means authority over paid search, local service ads, referral programs, and purchased leads, with a monthly reallocation rhythm rather than an annual plan.

### Vendor management

Agencies, lead providers, and software vendors should report to this person, not to the managing partner. The director sets targets, audits results, and ends contracts that miss. For the vendor side, see how to [choose a marketing agency](/blog/law-firm-marketing-agency) and hold it accountable.

### Attribution and reporting

One person must own the number that connects spend to signed retainers. Without it, every channel claims credit. Build this on a clear [marketing attribution model](/blog/law-firm-marketing-attribution) tied to the case management system.

### The marketing-to-intake handoff

This is where most hires fail. Marketing generates the call, intake converts it, and a director with no visibility into intake cannot diagnose the leak. Give the role read access to call recordings and conversion data, and a seat in the weekly intake review.

## Director, Manager, or Agency: Choosing the Right Level

Not every firm needs a director. Matching seniority to the actual problem avoids overpaying or underbuilding.

| Role | Best fit | Typical focus |
|---|---|---|
| Marketing coordinator | Firms under $10K monthly spend | Execution, content, directory upkeep |
| Marketing manager | $10K to $30K monthly spend | Channel performance, one or two vendors |
| Marketing director | $30K+ monthly spend, multiple practice areas | Strategy, budget, attribution, team leadership |
| Fractional director | Firms testing the role | Part-time strategy without full salary |

Compensation varies widely by market and practice area, so benchmark against current local listings rather than a national figure. Plan for a base salary plus a performance component tied to signed cases, and budget for the cost of a recruiting cycle as well.

## Building the Law Firm Marketing Director Role Around Signed Cases

Write the job around outcomes the managing partner already cares about. Activity metrics like posts published or keywords ranked are inputs, not results.

### Ninety-day expectations

In the first quarter, the director should deliver:

1. A single source of truth for leads, calls, and signed retainers by channel
2. An audit of every active vendor with a keep, fix, or cut recommendation
3. A cost per signed case baseline for each practice area
4. A documented handoff process between marketing and intake

### Metrics that hold the role accountable

Tie performance to numbers that trace to revenue. The [KPIs worth tracking](/blog/law-firm-kpis-to-track) include cost per signed case, lead-to-retainer conversion rate, and return on ad spend by channel.

Clio's [Legal Trends Report](https://www.clio.com/resources/legal-trends/) consistently shows that prospective clients contact multiple firms and favor those that respond quickly. A director who ignores response time is optimizing the wrong half of the funnel.

## Compliance Is Part of the Job

A marketing leader who has never read the advertising rules is a liability. The role should know the bar requirements in every state where the firm advertises, since the American Bar Association's [Model Rules on advertising and solicitation](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/professional_responsibility/publications/model_rules_of_professional_conduct/) set the baseline that state rules build on.

Responsibilities include:

- Reviewing ad copy, testimonials, and specialist claims before publication
- Documenting consent for calls, texts, and purchased leads
- Vetting lead vendors for how they source and verify data

The Thomson Reuters Institute [tracks how firms are investing in technology and talent](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en/institute.html), and compliance capability increasingly shows up as a differentiator.

## Common Mistakes When Hiring a Marketing Director

Avoid these patterns:

- **Hiring for brand when the problem is conversion.** A creative leader will not fix a leaking intake process.
- **Giving responsibility without budget authority.** If the director must ask a partner to move $5,000, the role is a coordinator with a better title.
- **Skipping the agency transition plan.** Existing vendors need a clear handoff, or the first quarter disappears into confusion.
- **Measuring on leads instead of cases.** Lead volume is easy to inflate and hard to monetize.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What does a law firm marketing director do?

A law firm marketing director owns the strategy, budget, vendor relationships, and reporting behind client acquisition. The role allocates spend across channels, tracks cost per signed case, and coordinates the handoff between marketing and intake. At larger firms they also manage a marketing team and ensure advertising complies with bar rules.

### When should a law firm hire a marketing director?

A law firm should hire a marketing director once monthly marketing spend reaches roughly $25,000 to $30,000 across several channels. Other signs include unclear channel attribution, partners micromanaging campaigns, and recurring disputes between marketing and intake. Below that spend level, an agency or fractional leader is usually more cost-effective.

### How much does a law firm marketing director cost?

Compensation depends on market, firm size, and practice area, so check current local job listings for accurate figures. Expect a base salary plus a bonus tied to signed cases, and add recruiting, benefits, and tool costs. A fractional director offers a lower-commitment way to test the role first.

### Should a law firm hire a marketing director or use an agency?

Hire a director when you need internal accountability across multiple vendors and channels, and use an agency for specialized execution. Many firms do both, with the director managing the agency against signed-case targets. An agency alone cannot reconcile its own results with intake and revenue data.

Most firms obsess over how fast the phone gets answered and ignore what happens the moment it does. A well-built **law firm intake script** is the difference between a caller who feels heard and qualified in ninety seconds and one who hangs up mid-sentence because the person on the line sounds like they're reading a form. Speed gets the prospect on the phone. The script decides whether they sign.

Firms that treat intake as a scripted, structured process consistently out-convert firms that leave it to instinct. According to the [Clio Legal Trends Report](https://www.clio.com/resources/legal-trends/), the majority of consumers contact multiple law firms before choosing one — meaning the firm with the tightest, most confident intake conversation often wins the case regardless of who answered first. A good law firm intake script is not a rigid checklist. It's a repeatable structure that trained staff can execute consistently, call after call, without sounding robotic.

## The Real Cost of a Weak Law Firm Intake Script

Untrained intake staff improvise. They ask questions out of order, forget to gather case-critical details, and let callers control the conversation instead of guiding it. The result shows up directly in your numbers.

Firms with no formal script typically see wide variance in conversion rates between staff members — one intake specialist signing 35% of qualified callers while another signs 15% on the same lead source. That gap isn't talent. It's process. A documented script closes it by giving every team member the same proven sequence of questions, the same objection responses, and the same path to a signed retainer.

The financial impact compounds fast. If your firm generates 200 qualified calls a month and a weak intake process converts 18% while a strong script converts 26%, that's 16 additional signed clients — without spending another dollar on advertising. Fixing the words matters as much as fixing the [speed to lead](/blog/speed-to-lead-law-firms).

## What a Law Firm Intake Script Should Actually Do

A script isn't meant to sound scripted. It's meant to make sure nothing important gets missed while the conversation still feels human. The best legal intake call scripts accomplish four things in every call:

- **Build rapport before asking for facts** — callers who have just been in an accident or are facing a legal problem need to feel heard before they'll answer detailed questions honestly.
- **Qualify the case against your firm's criteria** — statute of limitations, jurisdiction, liability clarity, and damages threshold, gathered in a consistent order.
- **Surface urgency and objections early** — is the caller also talking to other firms? Do they have a deadline? Getting this on the table lets your team respond instead of losing the case to a faster follow-up call from a competitor.
- **Close with a clear next step** — a scheduled consultation, a retainer sent for signature, or a specific callback time. Ambiguous endings are where signed cases quietly die.

## The Five-Part Framework for Legal Intake Call Scripts

Most high-performing legal intake call scripts follow a similar structure, regardless of practice area. The exact questions change for personal injury versus family law versus mass tort, but the shape stays consistent.

1. **Opening and empathy** — acknowledge what happened before asking anything else. "I'm sorry you're dealing with this — I'm going to ask a few questions so we can figure out the best way to help."
2. **Case-qualifying questions** — the specific facts that determine whether this is a case your firm takes: date of incident, injury or damages, other parties involved, prior legal representation.
3. **Disqualifying checks** — statute of limitations, conflicts of interest, jurisdiction. Catching these early saves your attorneys from reviewing files that were never viable.
4. **Value and urgency framing** — why acting now matters, without pressure tactics. This is where trained staff differ most from improvising staff.
5. **The ask** — a specific, confident next step, stated as a fact rather than a question. "I'm going to schedule you with [attorney] at 2pm today, and I'll text you the retainer right now."

### How to Qualify a Legal Client on the Phone

Qualifying is the part most scripts get wrong. Intake staff either ask too many questions upfront and lose the caller's patience, or too few and pass unqualified leads to attorneys who then waste billable time on calls that should have been screened out.

The fix is sequencing. Ask disqualifying questions first — the ones that determine whether this call is worth continuing — before asking for granular case detail. A workers' compensation intake, for example, should confirm the employer had coverage and the injury happened on the job before spending five minutes on medical history. This mirrors the logic behind [lead scoring](/blog/law-firm-lead-scoring): qualify fast, invest deeper only once the case clears the bar.

## Common Mistakes That Sink a Law Firm Intake Script

Even firms with a documented script undermine it in practice. The most frequent failures:

- **Reading the script verbatim.** Callers can tell. The script should be a mental checklist, not a teleprompter.
- **Skipping the empathy opening** to get to qualifying questions faster — this is the single fastest way to lose a caller who's already anxious.
- **No branching logic.** A script written for one practice area doesn't work for all of them; a personal injury script forced onto an employment law call misses the questions that actually matter.
- **No documented objection responses.** "I'm also talking to another firm" and "I need to think about it" come up constantly, and staff without a prepared response default to silence or pressure — both lose the case.
- **Treating the script as static.** Scripts that aren't reviewed against actual call outcomes drift out of date as case criteria and practice areas change.

## Training Your Intake Team to Use the Script Without Sounding Robotic

A script is only as good as the team executing it. Legal intake training should include live call review, not just a document handed to new hires on day one. Role-playing calls with real objections — the caller who's price-shopping, the caller who's hesitant to share details, the caller who's clearly not a fit — builds the judgment that separates a script-reader from a closer.

Firms that run weekly call reviews, where a supervisor listens to recorded calls against the script's five parts, see faster ramp times for new intake staff and fewer variances in individual conversion rates. This is the same discipline that underpins a well-structured [intake team](/blog/law-firm-intake-team-structure) — the script is the tool, but the training and accountability around it determine whether it actually gets used.

Pairing a documented script with a [legal CRM](/blog/legal-crm-for-law-firms) that prompts staff through each stage — rather than relying on memory — further reduces the gap between your best performer and your average one.

## Measuring Whether Your Script Is Working

A script isn't finished once it's written. Track it like any other conversion lever:

- **Conversion rate by intake staff member**, compared against calls with the same lead source and practice area.
- **Time-to-close** on qualified calls — a tight script should shorten the path from first contact to signed retainer.
- **Disqualification accuracy** — how often attorneys review a "qualified" case only to reject it, which signals the script's qualifying questions need tightening.

Firms that review this data monthly and adjust the script accordingly typically see conversion rates climb steadily over two to three quarters, the same pattern the [American Bar Association](https://www.americanbar.org/) has documented in firms that formalize client intake as a measured process rather than an ad hoc one. The [Thomson Reuters Institute](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en/institute.html) has similarly found that firms investing in structured intake processes report meaningfully higher realization rates on marketing spend, because fewer qualified leads are lost to inconsistent conversations. A script isn't a one-time project — it's a living document tied directly to your [conversion rate](/blog/law-firm-conversion-rate).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What should a law firm intake script include?

A law firm intake script should include an empathetic opening, case-qualifying questions specific to your practice area, disqualifying checks like statute of limitations and jurisdiction, urgency framing, and a clear, confident close. The strongest scripts are structured as a flexible checklist rather than word-for-word dialogue.

### How long should a legal intake call take?

Most qualifying legal intake calls run between eight and fifteen minutes, depending on practice area complexity. Mass tort and medical malpractice intake typically run longer due to the volume of case-specific detail required, while simpler practice areas like traffic or basic family law can qualify a caller in under ten minutes.

### How do you qualify a legal client on the phone?

You qualify a legal client on the phone by asking disqualifying questions first — statute of limitations, jurisdiction, coverage, and conflicts of interest — before spending time on detailed case history. This sequencing prevents wasted time on calls that were never going to convert while still building rapport with the caller.

### Should every intake staff member use the same script?

Yes, with room for practice-area branching. A consistent core structure across your intake team closes the performance gap between staff members and ensures every caller gets the same quality of qualifying conversation, regardless of who happens to answer.

### How often should a law firm update its intake script?

Review and update your intake script at least quarterly, or immediately after any change to case criteria, practice areas, or state regulations. Firms that track conversion data by script version catch drift early instead of losing signed cases to an outdated set of questions.

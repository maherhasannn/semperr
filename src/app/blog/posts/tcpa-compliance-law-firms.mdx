The Telephone Consumer Protection Act was written in 1991 to stop telemarketers from drowning consumers in robocalls. Decades later, it's become one of the most litigated federal statutes in the country — and law firms running outbound marketing campaigns are increasingly finding themselves on the wrong end of TCPA compliance lawsuits.

The irony is real: attorneys who handle TCPA class actions are also the attorneys most likely to violate TCPA rules in their own intake marketing. Understanding TCPA compliance for law firms isn't just about avoiding fines — it's about building a client acquisition operation that can scale without creating legal exposure for the firm itself.

## What TCPA Compliance Means for Law Firms

The TCPA prohibits making calls or sending texts to cell phones using an automatic telephone dialing system (ATDS) or a prerecorded message without prior express written consent. For law firms running high-volume intake operations — particularly in personal injury, mass tort, and workers' compensation — this governs directly how you contact prospective clients.

The penalties aren't trivial. Violations carry statutory damages of $500 per call or text, rising to $1,500 for willful violations. In a class action context, where a single marketing campaign may have reached thousands of people, exposure can climb into the millions.

TCPA compliance for law firms is complicated by the fact that many attorneys purchase leads from third-party vendors without asking hard questions about how those leads were generated or what consent language was actually used.

## The Consent Requirements You Actually Need

The FCC's [rules on TCPA consent](https://www.fcc.gov/consumers/guides/stop-unwanted-robocalls-and-texts) require that for marketing calls and texts to cell phones, firms must obtain **prior express written consent**. That means:

- A written agreement (electronic signatures count)
- A clear disclosure that the consumer agrees to receive autodialed calls or texts
- Disclosure of the specific entity that will be calling
- A statement that consent is not a condition of purchasing any goods or services

"Prior express consent" — a lower standard — applies to informational calls, but marketing communications require the written version. Many law firm intake teams conflate these two standards and create liability in the process.

One critical issue: consent obtained by a third-party lead vendor may not extend to your firm. If the vendor's consent form authorizes contact from "marketing partners" without naming your firm specifically, you are operating on legally shaky ground regardless of what the vendor claims.

## The FCC's 2024 Rule Change: One-to-One Consent

In December 2023, the FCC adopted a rule — effective January 2025 — requiring **one-to-one consent** for autodialed marketing communications. Under the old framework, a consumer could consent to contact from a broad category of "marketing partners." The new rule requires consent to be obtained separately for each company that intends to call.

This change fundamentally altered the lead generation landscape. Lead aggregators who sold the same consent record to multiple buyers now operate in a legally precarious position. Law firms that purchased such leads and continued calling are directly exposed.

If your firm buys leads from any third-party vendor, audit those vendor relationships now. Ask specifically:

1. Does the consumer consent form name your firm?
2. Was consent collected after January 2025 under the one-to-one standard?
3. What records does the vendor retain that demonstrate consent was obtained?

Vendors who cannot answer these questions clearly are a liability, not an asset. This is a core reason why [exclusive lead sourcing](/blog/exclusive-legal-leads) reduces legal marketing compliance risk — when your firm is the only buyer, the consent language can and should name you directly.

## Attorney Advertising Rules Add Another Layer

TCPA compliance for law firms doesn't operate in a vacuum. State bar rules on attorney advertising run parallel — and sometimes collide — with federal TCPA requirements.

While the TCPA governs the channel (phone calls, texts), the [ABA's Model Rules of Professional Conduct](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/professional_responsibility/publications/model_rules_of_professional_conduct/) govern the content of lawyer marketing communications. Model Rule 7.2 covers advertising broadly. Model Rule 7.3 addresses solicitation — direct contact with prospective clients for pecuniary gain.

Most states restrict real-time solicitation, require specific disclaimers, and prohibit misleading claims. A text message to a potential personal injury client might simultaneously violate TCPA (no written consent) and Rule 7.3 (improper solicitation). States like Florida and Texas have particularly strict rules on direct outreach following accidents.

A compliant attorney advertising compliance strategy requires review of both the channel rules and the content rules. Most firms treat these as separate workstreams and miss the combined exposure. ## How to Audit Your Current Lead Vendors

If your firm currently purchases leads from any external source, work through this audit before your next outbound campaign:

**Consent documentation:**
- Request copies of the actual consent forms used to generate your leads
- Verify the form names your firm or uses a mechanism that satisfies one-to-one standards
- Confirm consent records are retained and can be produced in litigation

**Data hygiene:**
- Scrub all phone numbers against the National Do Not Call Registry before outbound contact
- Cross-reference against your firm's internal DNC list
- Verify cell phone versus landline status — ATDS restrictions apply specifically to cell phones

**Vendor contracts:**
- Review indemnification clauses: does your vendor indemnify you for TCPA violations arising from their generation process?
- Check for representations and warranties about FCC compliance
- Understand what happens if a lead's consent is challenged in court

The [Clio Legal Trends Report](https://www.clio.com/resources/legal-trends/) consistently identifies client intake as one of the highest-friction areas in law firm operations. TCPA exposure makes a poorly structured intake process dramatically more expensive to operate and defend.

## TCPA Compliance Best Practices for Law Firm Outbound Campaigns

TCPA compliance is an operational discipline, not a one-time audit. For law firms running ongoing outbound intake, the process requirements look like this:

**Before contact:**
- Verify consent status for every record in your pipeline
- Confirm the number is not on federal or applicable state DNC lists
- Flag cell phones using a phone validation tool — do not assume landline

**During contact:**
- If using automated dialing or texting software, confirm the platform meets FCC requirements for your consent level
- Include required opt-out language in every text message ("Reply STOP to opt out")
- Train intake staff on FCC TCPA rules basics — live agent calls from manual dialing carry different requirements than autodialed communications

**After contact:**
- Log all consent and contact records with timestamps
- Honor opt-outs immediately and retain those records separately
- Keep all documentation for at least four years to cover the TCPA statute of limitations

Firms that invest in [optimizing their client intake process](/blog/legal-client-intake) build compliance infrastructure early — before scale creates systemic exposure. The disciplines overlap: a fast, well-structured intake operation and a compliant one are the same operation, built correctly from the start.

## The Real Cost of Non-Compliance

TCPA class actions are not hypothetical. In 2023 and 2024, plaintiffs' firms specifically targeting legal services companies filed TCPA suits against marketing vendors and their law firm clients. The structure is familiar: one plaintiff, a single compliance gap, a class certified on behalf of everyone who received similar non-compliant communications.

A single TCPA class action settlement can range from $500,000 to several million dollars — far exceeding any reasonable lead generation budget.

Beyond class actions, the FCC issues direct fines, and state attorneys general have grown more aggressive under state-level consumer protection statutes. California, Florida, and Illinois layer additional penalties on top of federal TCPA liability. For firms in high-volume practice areas like personal injury and mass torts, exposure multiplies with every campaign.

When you're modeling your [cost-per-case economics](/blog/law-firm-cost-per-case), TCPA compliance overhead belongs in the calculation. The firms that ignore it aren't saving money — they're deferring a far larger expense.

## What Compliant Lead Generation Actually Looks Like

The answer isn't to abandon outbound marketing. It's to source leads that come with provable, durable consent — and to work with vendors who can demonstrate their compliance posture, not just assert it.

Pre-qualified leads that satisfy TCPA compliance standards for law firms share a few defining characteristics:

- Consent is first-party and names your firm specifically
- Records are retained and producible on demand
- Contact happens within a time window where the prospect's intent is still fresh
- Leads are exclusive, so there is no ambiguity about who obtained what consent from whom

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What does TCPA compliance mean for law firms?

TCPA compliance for law firms means following FCC rules when making outbound calls or sending texts to prospective clients. Firms must obtain prior express written consent before using automated dialing systems or sending marketing texts to cell phones, maintain National Do Not Call Registry compliance, and retain consent records. Violations carry statutory penalties of $500–$1,500 per message.

### Can a law firm text prospective clients about their legal matter?

Yes, with the right consent in place. If a prospective client submitted a form expressing interest in your firm and the consent language covers text follow-up, that satisfies TCPA requirements for that contact. For outbound texts to purchased lists, prior express written consent that names your firm is required under the FCC's one-to-one consent rule, effective January 2025.

### What is the FCC's one-to-one consent rule and how does it affect lead buying?

The FCC's one-to-one consent rule requires that consent to receive autodialed marketing calls or texts be obtained separately for each company that intends to contact the consumer. This ended the practice of shared consent lists where a single form authorized contact from broad "marketing partner" categories. Law firms buying leads generated under pre-2025 shared consent frameworks face direct TCPA exposure on every call made using those records.

### Are bar association advertising rules separate from TCPA compliance?

Yes, but they overlap significantly in practice. TCPA is a federal statute governing the channel (phone, text) and applies to all businesses. Bar association advertising rules — based on ABA Model Rules 7.2 and 7.3 — govern the content and nature of attorney marketing specifically. A text message to a personal injury prospect can simultaneously violate TCPA and bar solicitation rules, creating dual regulatory exposure.

### How long should law firms retain TCPA consent records?

Best practice is to retain consent records for at least four years, which aligns with the TCPA's statute of limitations. Records should include the original consent form or screenshot, timestamp, IP address, and phone number. If consent was obtained through a third-party lead vendor, firms should contractually require that vendor to retain and produce records on demand.

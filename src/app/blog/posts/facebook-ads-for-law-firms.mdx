Most managing partners still treat Facebook as a branding channel, not a case-generation one. That's a mistake. **Facebook ads for law firms** now drive measurable case volume in personal injury, family law, employment, and mass tort—often at a lower cost per lead than Google Ads, because search intent is scarce and Meta's targeting can manufacture demand instead of waiting for it.

The catch is that most firms run Facebook ads the way they'd run a billboard: broad targeting, generic copy, no compliance review, and no real funnel behind the click. That's why so many attorneys conclude "Facebook doesn't work for lawyers." Done well, Facebook ads for law firms work. They're just being used wrong most of the time.

## Why Facebook Ads for Law Firms Still Work in 2026

Search advertising captures people who already know they need a lawyer. Meta's platform reaches people before that realization fully forms—someone scrolling who just got a diagnosis, just got served divorce papers, or just watched a coworker get fired without cause.

That's a fundamentally different job than Google Ads does, and it's why the two channels shouldn't be judged by the same metrics. A [law firm's PPC advertising](/blog/law-firm-ppc-advertising) strategy captures existing demand; Meta ads create awareness and pre-qualify interest before the prospect ever searches. That distinction matters more as client acquisition costs climb across the industry—the [Clio Legal Trends Report](https://www.clio.com/resources/legal-trends/) has tracked rising competition for the same search traffic every firm is chasing on Google.

Three shifts have made this channel more viable for firms specifically:

- **Lead form ads** now let prospects submit contact information without leaving the platform, cutting drop-off compared to sending traffic to a landing page.
- **Advantage+ campaign types** use Meta's automated targeting to find converting audiences faster than manual interest-based targeting did in prior years.
- **Video and Reels placements** reward attorneys who show up on camera, which builds the trust signal that written ad copy alone can't replicate. That trend connects directly to what's working in [law firm video marketing](/blog/law-firm-video-marketing) more broadly.

## Facebook Ads for Law Firms: Campaign Types That Convert

Not every campaign objective performs the same for legal services. Three formats consistently produce case-worthy leads from Facebook ads for law firms:

1. **Lead generation campaigns** using Meta's native lead forms, pre-qualified with 2-3 screening questions (injury type, date of incident, whether they've already spoken to another attorney).
2. **Traffic campaigns to a dedicated landing page**, used when the case type requires more explanation than a lead form allows—mass tort intake is the clearest example.
3. **Retargeting campaigns** aimed at people who visited the firm's site but didn't convert, which typically produce the lowest cost-per-lead of any campaign type because the audience already has intent.

Awareness and engagement campaigns—boosted posts, page-like ads—rarely produce cases directly. They're useful for brand building but shouldn't be measured against intake numbers.

## Targeting Options That Actually Reach Prospective Clients

Meta's targeting has narrowed considerably since interest-based targeting was scaled back for privacy reasons, but firms still have real levers:

- **Geographic radius targeting** around the firm's service area, refined by zip code density rather than broad city-level targeting.
- **Custom audiences** built from the firm's existing client list and call data, used to build lookalike audiences that resemble past signed clients.
- **Life-event and demographic signals**, which remain available and are especially relevant for family law, employment, and estate planning campaigns.
- **Advantage+ audience expansion**, which lets Meta's algorithm find converting users the firm's manual targeting would have missed.

Custom audiences built from actual case data consistently outperform interest-based targeting alone. A firm that feeds its CRM data into Meta's audience tools is targeting people who resemble clients who already signed—not just people who clicked "like" on a personal injury page.

## What Facebook Ads Cost for Law Firms in 2026

Cost-per-lead on Meta for legal services typically runs lower than Google Ads, but conversion rates from lead to signed case tend to run lower too, since Meta users have less immediate intent than searchers.

Rough benchmarks firms are seeing this year:

- **Personal injury**: $35-$90 per lead, depending on metro competition
- **Family law**: $25-$60 per lead
- **Employment law**: $30-$70 per lead
- **Mass tort intake**: $15-$45 per lead, reflecting the higher volume, lower-qualification nature of tort campaigns

These numbers only tell half the story. What matters is cost per *signed case*, not cost per lead—and that number depends entirely on intake speed and screening quality, which is where most firms actually lose money on this channel. For firms trying to model the full picture, [law firm cost per case](/blog/law-firm-cost-per-case) analysis is the right framework to apply before scaling spend on Facebook ads for law firms.

## Compliance Rules Attorneys Can't Ignore on Meta

Bar advertising rules apply to Facebook and Instagram exactly as they apply to any other attorney advertising. Meta's own ad review process doesn't check for bar compliance—that responsibility sits entirely with the firm.

The most common violations firms make on Meta:

- Testimonials or case results in ad creative without required disclaimers
- Guarantees of outcome ("we'll win your case") in ad copy
- Missing "Attorney Advertising" disclosures where a firm's home state requires them
- Targeting minors or using imagery that implies solicitation of a specific injured person

Every firm running paid social should have creative reviewed against the same standard used for [attorney advertising compliance](/blog/attorney-advertising-compliance) across other channels, including the disclosure and disclaimer requirements laid out in [ABA Model Rule 7.1](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/professional_responsibility/publications/model_rules_of_professional_conduct/rule_7_1_communications_concerning_a_lawyers_services_information_about_legal_services/). A compliant ad that runs for months beats a high-performing ad that triggers a bar complaint and gets pulled.

## Facebook Ads vs. Google Ads vs. Local Services Ads

None of these channels should be evaluated as a single winner. Each captures a different point in the client's decision process.

Google Ads captures people actively searching for a lawyer right now—highest intent, highest cost. [Local Services Ads for law firms](/blog/local-services-ads-for-law-firms) captures a similar intent level but with a pay-per-lead model instead of pay-per-click. Facebook ads capture people who haven't started searching yet but match the profile of someone who will need representation soon.

Firms that run all three in parallel typically see Meta perform as a volume and awareness driver, with paid search closing a higher percentage of what it touches. Treating Facebook as a replacement for search spend—rather than a complement to it—is the single biggest strategic error firms make when they first test the channel.

## Building a Facebook Ads Funnel That Feeds Intake

An ad that generates a lead is worthless if intake can't act on it fast enough. Meta leads convert on contact speed even more than search leads do, because the prospect wasn't actively looking for a lawyer when they clicked.

The funnel that works:

1. **Ad click** to lead form or landing page, with copy that speaks to the specific case type—not generic "call now" messaging
2. **Instant notification** to intake staff or a dialer system the moment a lead form submits
3. **Contact within 5 minutes**, which is the threshold where conversion rates start dropping sharply for cold social leads
4. **CRM tracking** that attributes the case back to the specific ad, audience, and campaign that produced it

Firms without this infrastructure in place waste most of what they spend on Meta, regardless of how well the campaigns are targeted. Getting this funnel right is usually the difference between Facebook ads for law firms that pay for themselves and campaigns that get shut off after one disappointing quarter. Research from the [Thomson Reuters Institute](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en/institute.html) has repeatedly found that response speed, not just marketing spend, is one of the strongest predictors of whether a legal lead converts into a signed client.

## Common Mistakes That Waste Ad Spend

The firms that give up on Facebook ads for law firms after one bad quarter usually made one of these errors:

- **Sending traffic to a homepage** instead of a case-specific landing page built for the ad's message
- **Ignoring frequency caps**, letting the same audience see the same ad until performance decays
- **Skipping A/B testing** on creative, running one ad set indefinitely instead of testing hooks
- **Measuring cost-per-lead instead of cost-per-case**, which hides whether the leads are actually converting
- **Letting leads sit unworked** for hours, which guarantees a low answer rate regardless of ad quality

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do Facebook ads work for law firms?

Yes, particularly for personal injury, family law, employment law, and mass tort intake, where Meta's targeting can reach people before they start actively searching for an attorney. Performance depends heavily on landing page quality, lead form screening, and how fast intake responds to new leads.

### How much do Facebook ads cost for law firms?

Cost per lead typically ranges from $15 for high-volume mass tort campaigns to $90 for competitive personal injury markets. The more useful metric is cost per signed case, which depends on lead qualification and intake speed rather than the ad spend alone.

### Are Facebook ads better than Google Ads for lawyers?

Neither replaces the other—they capture different stages of intent. Google Ads reaches people actively searching for representation, while Facebook ads reach people who match a converting client profile before they've started that search. Most firms see the best results running both.

### What are the compliance risks with Facebook ads for attorneys?

The same bar advertising rules that apply to any attorney marketing apply to Meta ads, including disclaimer requirements, restrictions on outcome guarantees, and state-specific "Attorney Advertising" disclosures. Meta's ad review does not check for bar compliance, so firms are responsible for reviewing their own creative.

### What campaign type works best for law firm Facebook ads?

Lead generation campaigns using Meta's native lead forms typically perform best for straightforward case types, while traffic campaigns to a dedicated landing page work better for cases requiring more explanation, such as mass tort. Retargeting campaigns aimed at prior site visitors usually produce the lowest cost per lead.

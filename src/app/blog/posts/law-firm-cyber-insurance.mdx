Most managing partners assume their malpractice policy handles a breach. It usually does not. Law firm cyber insurance exists because lawyers' professional liability coverage is built to defend claims from clients, not to pay for the forensic investigators, notification letters, ransom negotiations, and lost billing days that pile up in the first two weeks of an incident.

The second surprise comes later. Firms that do buy a policy often discover at claim time that an answer on the application, an unapproved vendor, or a sublimit they never read has cut the payout to a fraction of the loss.

## Why Law Firm Cyber Insurance Is Not the Same as Malpractice Coverage

A lawyers' professional liability policy responds when a client alleges you made an error in rendering legal services. A breach generates that kind of claim eventually, but most of the money goes out the door long before anyone sues.

Those early expenses are **first-party costs**: the firm's own losses, not a third party's demand. Many malpractice policies exclude them outright or cap them with a small endorsement that would not cover a single week of incident response.

The exposure is not hypothetical. The [ABA's Legal Technology Survey](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/law_practice/resources/tech-report/) found that 29 percent of responding firms had experienced a security breach at some point. IBM's 2025 Cost of a Data Breach Report put the average US breach at $10.22 million, the highest of any country it measured.

Plaintiff firms carry a specific kind of risk. Medical records, Social Security numbers, settlement figures, and bank details sit in the same systems as thousands of prospect records from people who never signed, which makes the record count far larger than the active caseload suggests.

## What a Cyber Policy Actually Covers

Law firm cyber insurance varies by carrier, but nearly every policy is assembled from the same three blocks. Read each one separately, because the limits and deductibles often differ block by block.

### First-party coverage

This is the part that pays the firm's own bills:

- **Incident response:** breach counsel, forensic investigation, and crisis communications
- **Notification and monitoring:** letters to affected individuals, call center support, and credit monitoring
- **Business interruption:** lost income and extra expense while systems are down
- **Data restoration:** rebuilding or recovering corrupted files and systems
- **Cyber extortion:** the cost of responding to a ransom demand, which is where ransomware coverage lives

### Third-party coverage

This block defends the firm against claims brought by others. It covers privacy liability suits from clients or prospects, regulatory investigations and fines where insurable, and payment card assessments if the firm takes cards.

It overlaps with malpractice coverage at the edges. When both policies could respond, the "other insurance" clauses decide who pays first, and that fight is easier to settle before a loss than during one.

### Crime and social engineering coverage

For law firms this is the block that matters most and gets read least. It covers funds transfer fraud, where an attacker spoofs a client, opposing counsel, or a lienholder and redirects a settlement wire.

Social engineering coverage is frequently sublimited to $100,000 or $250,000 even on a policy with a multimillion-dollar aggregate. One misdirected settlement disbursement can exceed that, and the firm still owes the client the money under its trust accounting duties.

## What Underwriters Require Before They Will Quote

The market for law firm cyber insurance tightened sharply after the ransomware wave of 2020 and 2021. Carriers now treat the application as a security audit, and a "no" on the wrong question means a declined quote or a ransomware sublimit.

Standard cyber insurance requirements now include:

1. **Multi-factor authentication** on email, remote access, and administrator accounts
2. **Endpoint detection and response** software on every workstation and server
3. **Offline or immutable backups** that are tested on a schedule
4. **Patch management** with critical updates applied within a defined window
5. **Security awareness training** and phishing simulations for all staff
6. **A written incident response plan** that names who calls whom
7. **Callback verification** for any change to wire instructions

Most of these overlap with what the ethics rules already expect. [Model Rule 1.6(c)](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/professional_responsibility/publications/model_rules_of_professional_conduct/rule_1_6_confidentiality_of_information/) requires reasonable efforts to prevent unauthorized access to client information, and the baseline controls in our guide to [law firm cybersecurity compliance](/blog/law-firm-cybersecurity-compliance) map closely to an underwriter's checklist.

Answer the application precisely. In *Travelers v. International Control Services*, the carrier sued to rescind a policy after a ransomware attack, alleging the insured had claimed MFA protection it did not fully have. The policy was voided by agreement.

Whoever signs the application should confirm each answer with IT in writing. "We have MFA" and "we have MFA on every account the question asks about" are different statements.

## The Exclusions That Sink Law Firm Cyber Insurance Claims

Law firm cyber insurance is defined as much by what it refuses to pay as by what it covers. These are the provisions that produce the most denied or reduced claims for firms.

- **Wrongful collection exclusions.** Many policies exclude claims arising from how data was gathered rather than how it was lost. That removes coverage for pixel-tracking, wiretapping, biometric, and TCPA suits, which are the claims a marketing-heavy firm is most likely to face.
- **Failure to maintain security.** Some forms exclude losses if the firm let the controls described in its application lapse.
- **Retroactive dates.** An intrusion that began before the retro date may not be covered, even if it was discovered during the policy period.
- **Unapproved vendors.** Hiring your own forensic firm or paying a ransom without the carrier's consent can forfeit reimbursement.
- **Dependent business interruption.** If your case management or phone vendor goes down, you are covered only if the policy extends to outages at third parties.
- **War and state-backed attacks.** Newer forms carve out incidents attributed to nation-state actors.

The wrongful collection exclusion deserves attention from any firm running paid traffic. A cyber policy will not rescue a firm from a [CIPA wiretapping claim](/blog/cipa-compliance-law-firms) over a chat widget, so that risk has to be managed through consent design, not insurance.

The dependent business interruption gap is the other one to close. Most intake stacks run on a handful of outside platforms, and the inventory you build for [vendor risk management](/blog/law-firm-vendor-risk-management) doubles as the list of outages you need the policy to cover.

## How Much Coverage to Buy and What It Costs

Cyber insurance cost for law firms depends on revenue, record count, practice areas, controls, and claims history. A solo or small firm with clean controls can often find a $1 million policy in the low four figures per year. Firms holding large volumes of medical or financial data pay meaningfully more.

Do not pick a law firm cyber insurance limit by rounding. Size it from three numbers:

- **Record count.** Include every prospect in the CRM, not just signed clients. Old leads you never purged are still notification obligations, which is one more argument for enforcing a [data retention policy](/blog/legal-data-retention-policy).
- **Daily revenue at risk.** Estimate what two to three weeks without email, phones, and case files would cost in missed intake and delayed settlements.
- **Largest routine wire.** Your social engineering sublimit should at least match the biggest disbursement your trust account sends in a normal month.

Contingency firms face a wrinkle on the business interruption number. Carriers calculate lost income from historical revenue, and lumpy settlement income makes that calculation easy to dispute.

Ask the broker how the policy measures loss for a firm whose revenue arrives in irregular settlements. If the answer is vague, get the methodology endorsed in writing or expect an argument at claim time.

## What to Do in the First Hour of a Claim

The policy dictates the response, so the first call goes to the carrier's breach hotline. That call usually triggers assignment of breach counsel, who then retains the forensic firm so the investigation runs under privilege.

Acting first and notifying later is the most common way firms damage their own claims. Wiping machines destroys evidence, hiring an unapproved vendor creates unreimbursed bills, and late notice hands the carrier a defense.

Law firm cyber insurance does not replace the firm's legal duties. State notification statutes and the ethical obligation to tell affected clients still run on their own clocks, as outlined in our guide to [law firm data breach notification](/blog/law-firm-data-breach-notification).

Keep the policy number, hotline, and broker contact printed and stored offline. If ransomware has encrypted the document system, a PDF of the policy inside it is no help.

## Coverage Starts With the Data You Choose to Hold

Underwriters price law firm cyber insurance on what a firm stores and how it got there. Leads with documented consent, clear sourcing, and defined retention periods are easier to insure and cheaper to notify on than a CRM full of records nobody can trace.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does malpractice insurance cover a law firm data breach?

Usually not in full. Malpractice policies respond to client claims alleging professional error, and most exclude or tightly sublimit first-party breach costs such as forensics, notification, ransom payments, and lost income. Law firm cyber insurance is designed to pay those costs directly.

### How much does cyber insurance cost for law firms?

Cost depends on revenue, the number of records held, security controls, and claims history. Small firms with strong controls can often buy a $1 million policy for a low four-figure annual premium, while firms with large volumes of medical or financial data pay more. Missing controls such as multi-factor authentication raise the price or block the quote entirely.

### What are the cyber insurance requirements for a law firm?

Most carriers require multi-factor authentication, endpoint detection software, tested offline backups, regular patching, staff security training, and a written incident response plan. Many also ask about callback procedures for wire transfers. Inaccurate answers on the application can give the carrier grounds to rescind the policy after a loss.

### Does cyber liability insurance for attorneys cover wire fraud from a trust account?

Only if the policy includes social engineering or funds transfer fraud coverage, and often only up to a sublimit. That sublimit is commonly far lower than the policy's overall limit, so firms should compare it against their largest routine disbursement. Many policies also require documented callback verification before they will pay.

### Is ransomware coverage included in a standard cyber policy?

Most policies include cyber extortion coverage, but carriers frequently apply a separate sublimit or coinsurance to ransomware losses. Coverage typically requires the carrier's consent before any payment is made. Firms without tested backups or multi-factor authentication may find ransomware coverage reduced or excluded.

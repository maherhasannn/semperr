Most managing partners set their **law firm marketing budget** the same way every year: take last year's number, adjust it up or down based on gut feeling, and move on. That approach works fine when the market is flat. It fails badly when a competitor doubles their spend in your practice area, or when case costs climb 20% and nobody adjusts the plan to match.

The firms that grow predictably don't guess at their budget. They build it from a formula tied to revenue targets, practice area economics, and channel performance — then revisit it quarterly instead of annually. This guide breaks down exactly how to do that, with real benchmarks you can use today.

## What Should Your Law Firm Marketing Budget Actually Be?

The standard answer from most consultants is "7% to 12% of gross revenue," borrowed from general small-business marketing guidance. For law firms, that range is too generic to be useful. Legal marketing spend behaves differently than retail or SaaS because case value, sales cycle length, and competitive intensity vary enormously by practice area.

According to the [Clio Legal Trends Report](https://www.clio.com/resources/legal-trends/), legal marketing spend as a share of revenue commonly ranges from 2% to 18%, with high-volume consumer practices like personal injury and mass tort sitting at the top of that range and transactional or advisory practices sitting near the bottom.

A more useful starting formula ties budget to growth ambition rather than a flat percentage:

- **Maintenance budget (flat growth):** 5–8% of gross revenue
- **Moderate growth budget (10–20% YoY growth target):** 8–15% of gross revenue
- **Aggressive growth budget (25%+ YoY growth target):** 15–25% of gross revenue

New practices and firms entering a competitive market for the first time should expect to sit at the higher end of these ranges for at least 12–18 months before efficiency improves. If you're weighing whether to enter a new practice area entirely, budget planning for that move should happen alongside the decision itself, not after the launch is already underway.

## Marketing Budget Benchmarks by Practice Area

Practice area is the single biggest driver of what a reasonable law firm advertising budget looks like, because it determines both competitive intensity and case value.

**Rough annual marketing spend as a percentage of revenue, by practice area:**

- **Personal injury:** 12–20%, among the highest of any practice area due to intense paid search and lead vendor competition
- **Mass tort:** 15–25%, driven by inventory acquisition costs and compressed litigation timelines
- **Family law:** 6–10%, lower competitive intensity but longer nurture cycles
- **Criminal defense:** 5–9%, heavily local and often relationship-driven
- **Workers' compensation:** 8–14%, competitive in urban markets, lower in rural ones
- **Estate planning / trusts:** 4–7%, longer sales cycles, higher reliance on referrals

These figures assume a firm buying media and leads through standard digital channels. Firms relying more heavily on referral networks can often run 2–4 percentage points lower, though referral-dependent growth is harder to scale predictably. ## How to Build a Law Firm Marketing Budget That Scales With Growth

Percentage-of-revenue formulas are a starting point, not a plan. A durable budget is built bottom-up from your case goals, then checked against the top-down percentage as a sanity test.

**Step 1: Set a case volume target.** Decide how many signed cases you need this year to hit your revenue goal, broken out by practice area if you run more than one.

**Step 2: Apply your current cost per case.** Multiply target case volume by your known (or benchmarked) cost per case. If you don't have a reliable number yet, our guide on [law firm cost per case](/blog/law-firm-cost-per-case) walks through the calculation.

**Step 3: Add a buffer for channel inefficiency.** New channels rarely perform at mature-channel efficiency in year one. Add 15–25% to your calculated number if you're launching a new channel or entering a new market.

**Step 4: Reconcile against the percentage-of-revenue range.** If your bottom-up number falls wildly outside the benchmark range for your practice area, that's a signal — either your cost-per-case assumption is off, or your growth target is unrealistic for the budget you're willing to commit.

This bottom-up approach also forces a conversation most firms avoid: whether the growth target is actually affordable. A firm chasing 30% growth on an 8% marketing budget is setting itself up to underspend against the plan by mid-year, which is one of the most common reasons a law firm marketing budget gets blown through before Q4.

## Where the Budget Should Go: Channel Allocation

Once the total number is set, allocation matters as much as the size of the budget. A common mistake is spreading spend evenly across channels instead of weighting toward what's proven to convert.

A reasonable starting allocation for a mid-sized consumer practice:

1. **Paid search and lead acquisition (40–55%)** — the largest line item for most high-volume practices, covering PPC and vendor-sourced leads
2. **SEO and organic content (10–20%)** — compounding return but slower to materialize; underfunding this channel is the most common long-term mistake
3. **Intake and conversion infrastructure (10–15%)** — CRM, call tracking, and intake staffing, often excluded from "marketing" budgets entirely but essential to realizing ROI on the spend above
4. **Brand and local presence (10–15%)** — reviews, local listings, sponsorships, and community visibility
5. **Testing and emerging channels (5–10%)** — video, social, and other channels still proving out for your market

## Common Law Firm Marketing Budget Mistakes

Even a well-researched law firm marketing budget gets undermined by a handful of recurring errors.

- **Treating the budget as fixed for the full year.** Markets shift mid-year — a competitor's spend surge or a cost-per-lead spike should trigger a budget review, not a wait-and-see approach until next January.
- **Excluding intake labor from the total.** If your marketing budget only counts media spend and ignores the staff needed to convert leads into signed cases, you're underfunding the function that actually determines ROI.
- **Cutting budget the moment cost per case rises.** A temporary CPC increase often reflects a competitive market shift, not a broken channel. Cutting spend reflexively can shrink your case pipeline right when competitors are investing to capture share.
- **No reserve for testing.** Firms that allocate 100% of budget to proven channels stop finding new growth levers. A small testing allocation, even 5%, keeps the channel mix from stagnating.

The [Thomson Reuters Institute](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en/institute.html) has noted that firms with formal, revisited marketing budgets consistently outperform firms operating on ad hoc spend decisions, particularly during periods of rising acquisition costs.

## Adjusting Budget by Growth Stage

A law firm marketing budget should look different depending on where the firm sits in its growth curve — a mature practice with a strong referral base doesn't need the same spend intensity as a firm two years into a new market.

**Early-stage firms (0–3 years in market):** Expect to run at the top of your practice area's benchmark range. Brand awareness and case volume both need to be built from close to zero, and case quality data is too thin yet to optimize efficiently.

**Established firms (4–10 years):** Budget should stabilize toward the middle of the range, with a higher share shifted toward SEO, referral development, and retention marketing as brand equity compounds.

**Mature firms (10+ years):** The lowest sustainable percentage of revenue, provided referral flow and organic visibility are strong. Budget increases at this stage are usually tied to specific expansion moves — a new practice area or new geography — rather than baseline maintenance.

Firms navigating a stage transition, especially expansion into a new practice area, should treat the budget shift as a discrete project with its own ROI timeline rather than folding it into the existing line item. ## Getting the Number Right Matters More Than the Number Itself

There's no universal correct answer to "how much should a law firm spend on marketing." The right number depends on practice area, growth ambition, and how efficiently your intake process converts what the budget buys. What matters more than hitting a specific percentage is building the number from actual case economics and revisiting it as those economics change.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much should a law firm spend on marketing?

Most law firms should budget between 5% and 20% of gross revenue on marketing, depending on practice area and growth targets. High-volume consumer practices like personal injury and mass tort typically sit at the higher end, while advisory practices like estate planning run lower. The right figure is best built bottom-up from case volume goals and cost per case, then checked against these benchmarks.

### What percentage of revenue should a law firm marketing budget be?

A maintenance-level marketing budget typically runs 5–8% of gross revenue, while firms pursuing aggressive growth targets often allocate 15–25%. Practice area competitiveness is the biggest variable — personal injury and mass tort firms routinely spend above 15%, while transactional practices can operate effectively closer to 5%.

### How do I calculate my law firm's marketing budget?

Start with your target case volume for the year, multiply it by your known or benchmarked cost per case, and add a 15–25% buffer if you're launching new channels or entering a new market. Then check that bottom-up number against practice-area percentage-of-revenue benchmarks as a sanity check before finalizing it.

### Should intake staffing be included in the marketing budget?

Yes. Intake labor directly determines how much value the firm captures from every dollar spent on lead generation, so excluding it understates the true cost of acquisition and can lead to underfunding the conversion side of the funnel. Firms that budget for media spend alone consistently misjudge their real return on marketing investment.

### How often should a law firm review its marketing budget?

Quarterly at minimum, with a lighter monthly check on cost-per-case trends. Markets, competitor spend, and channel performance shift often enough that an annual-only review leaves firms reacting to problems months after they start, rather than adjusting proactively.

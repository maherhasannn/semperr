Most intake managers can recite their TCPA obligations in their sleep. Ask the same person about **law firm Do Not Call compliance** under the FTC's Telemarketing Sales Rule, and you'll usually get a blank stare. That gap is a problem, because the two rulebooks are not interchangeable — and a firm that's fully TCPA-compliant can still be violating Do Not Call rules on every live-agent call its intake team makes.

The confusion is understandable. TCPA compliance dominates the legal marketing conversation because it carries statutory damages and fuels class actions. But the Telemarketing Sales Rule, enforced by the FTC and the National Do Not Call Registry, governs a different piece of the same process: the live phone calls intake staff place to people who submitted a form, requested a callback, or showed up as a purchased lead. If your firm calls consumers, law firm Do Not Call compliance is not optional, and it doesn't disappear just because the call wasn't autodialed.

## What Law Firm Do Not Call Compliance Actually Requires

The National Do Not Call Registry lets consumers opt out of telemarketing calls entirely. Once a phone number is registered, telemarketers — a category that includes law firms placing outbound calls tied to a commercial purpose — generally cannot call it, regardless of where the lead came from.

There are two carve-outs that matter for legal intake:

- **Established business relationship (EBR).** If a consumer has an existing relationship with your firm — a prior client, someone who scheduled a consultation — you can typically call for up to 18 months after the relationship ends.
- **Express written consent.** A consumer who affirmatively agreed to receive calls from your firm specifically, in writing, can be called even if their number is on the registry.

A purchased lead usually satisfies neither carve-out on its own. A form-fill on a lead vendor's website is not an established business relationship with your firm, and generic consent to be contacted by "partners" doesn't meet the specificity bar regulators expect. This is the same gap that creates exposure under [TCPA compliance for law firms](/blog/tcpa-compliance-law-firms), and it's worth treating both rules as a single vendor due diligence problem rather than two separate checklists.

## The National Do Not Call Registry vs. TCPA: Two Different Rulebooks

Firms conflate these two regimes constantly, and the confusion creates blind spots. The distinction is simple once it's stated plainly:

1. **TCPA** governs *how* a call or text is placed — autodialers, prerecorded messages, and SMS. It requires prior express written consent for marketing communications to cell phones and carries private-right-of-action damages of $500–$1,500 per violation.
2. **The Telemarketing Sales Rule (TSR)**, enforced by the FTC, governs telemarketing calls broadly, including calls placed manually by a live agent. It requires firms to honor Do Not Call registrations, maintain an internal do-not-call list, and disclose the calling entity's identity.

A live agent dialing manually from a purchased lead list can be fully outside TCPA's autodialer provisions and still violate the TSR if that number is registered and no exemption applies. Law firm Do Not Call compliance has to be evaluated independently of TCPA — treating them as the same obligation is the single most common mistake intake compliance teams make.

Required call disclosures under the TSR — caller identity, the purpose of the call, and the firm's name — sit alongside a similar obligation in the [ABA Model Rules of Professional Conduct](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/professional_responsibility/publications/model_rules_of_professional_conduct/), where Rule 7.3 governs live solicitation of prospective clients. A call that satisfies TSR disclosure requirements but violates a state's solicitation rules is still a compliance failure, just under a different rulebook.

## Where Purchased Leads Create the Most Exposure

Lead vendors rarely disclose Do Not Call scrubbing practices unprompted, and firms rarely ask. That silence is where most of the risk lives, and it's a growing one — the [Clio Legal Trends Report](https://www.clio.com/resources/legal-trends/) has tracked a steady rise in firms outsourcing client acquisition to third-party channels, which means more purchased data reaching intake teams with no visibility into how it was scrubbed.

- **No registry scrubbing.** Some vendors sell raw form-fill data without cross-referencing it against the National Do Not Call Registry at all.
- **Stale consent records.** A consumer's registry status can change after a lead was generated; a list that was clean in January may not be clean by the time your team calls in March.
- **Vague consent language.** "I agree to be contacted by marketing partners" does not establish the specific, documented relationship the TSR's safe harbors require.
- **No audit trail.** If a firm can't produce the vendor's consent record on demand, it can't defend a complaint — regardless of whether the vendor's practices were actually compliant.

This is precisely the due diligence gap covered in our guide to [data broker compliance for law firms](/blog/data-broker-compliance-law-firms): a vendor's pricing and lead quality matter, but their consent documentation is what actually protects your firm when a complaint lands. Firms that rely on [exclusive legal leads](/blog/exclusive-legal-leads) rather than shared, resold data have a structural advantage here, because consent language can be written to name the buying firm directly rather than an undefined pool of "partners."

## The Abandoned Call Rate Rule Most Intake Teams Ignore

Beyond registry scrubbing, the TSR imposes an **abandoned call rate** requirement that predictive dialers and high-volume intake teams routinely violate without realizing it. Firms using automated or predictive dialing systems to work through purchased lead lists must keep abandoned calls — calls connected to a live person but not answered by an agent within two seconds — under 3% of all calls answered, measured over a 30-day rolling period.

Intake teams under pressure to hit speed-to-lead benchmarks sometimes over-provision dialers to maximize agent efficiency, which pushes the abandoned call rate up without anyone tracking it. The fix isn't complicated, but it requires deliberate monitoring:

1. Pull abandoned call rate reports from your dialer platform monthly, not annually.
2. Cap simultaneous outbound lines relative to available agents.
3. Play the required identification message on any call that does go unanswered by an agent within the rule's two-second window.

Fast response still matters enormously for conversion — see our breakdown of [speed to lead for law firms](/blog/speed-to-lead-law-firms) — but speed built on a non-compliant dialing configuration just trades one problem for a bigger one.

## Building a Law Firm Do Not Call Compliance Checklist

A workable law firm Do Not Call compliance program doesn't require a legal department. It requires a documented process that intake leadership actually follows:

- **Scrub every purchased list** against the National Do Not Call Registry before it reaches an agent's dialer, and re-scrub lists older than 31 days.
- **Maintain an internal do-not-call list** and honor removal requests immediately, independent of the national registry.
- **Require vendor consent documentation** as a condition of any purchase agreement — not a courtesy the vendor provides if asked.
- **Train agents on required disclosures**: the caller's identity, the firm's name, and the purpose of the call within the opening seconds.
- **Log call outcomes and consent status** for every lead in your CRM, mirroring the approach outlined in [our law firm intake script guide](/blog/law-firm-intake-script), so the compliance record and the sales record live in the same system.

Firms that already built out consent tracking for TCPA purposes are close to TSR compliance too — the systems overlap heavily. The gap is almost always the registry-scrubbing step, which many firms assume their vendor already handled.

## What Happens When Firms Get This Wrong

TSR enforcement runs through the FTC and state attorneys general, and penalties can reach over $50,000 per violation in egregious cases — separate from any private TCPA exposure on the same calls. State-level Do Not Call statutes, several of which are stricter than the federal registry, add another layer most national campaigns overlook entirely.

The reputational cost compounds the financial one. Legal industry coverage from the [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com) has tracked a steady increase in state attorneys general pursuing telemarketing enforcement actions against professional services firms, a trend that shows no sign of slowing as intake operations scale up outbound calling volume.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is law firm Do Not Call compliance under the Telemarketing Sales Rule?

Law firm Do Not Call compliance means honoring the National Do Not Call Registry, maintaining an internal do-not-call list, and disclosing caller identity on every outbound telemarketing call, as required by the FTC's Telemarketing Sales Rule. It applies to live-agent calls, not just autodialed ones, which makes it distinct from TCPA.

### Is the Telemarketing Sales Rule different from TCPA?

Yes. TCPA governs autodialers, prerecorded messages, and texts to cell phones, while the Telemarketing Sales Rule governs telemarketing calls broadly, including manually dialed calls, and enforces the National Do Not Call Registry. A firm can be TCPA-compliant and still violate the TSR.

### Can a law firm call a purchased lead that's on the Do Not Call Registry?

Only if an exemption applies, such as an established business relationship or documented express written consent naming the firm specifically. Generic consent to be contacted by "marketing partners" typically does not meet that bar, which is why vendor consent documentation matters before any purchased lead is dialed.

### What is the abandoned call rate rule?

The Telemarketing Sales Rule requires that no more than 3% of calls answered by a live person go unanswered by an agent within two seconds, measured over a rolling 30-day period. Firms using predictive dialers on high-volume lead lists need to monitor this monthly to stay compliant.

### How can a law firm verify a lead vendor's Do Not Call compliance?

Require the vendor to provide documented consent language, the date consent was collected, and confirmation that the list was scrubbed against the National Do Not Call Registry before delivery. A vendor that can't produce this on request is a compliance liability regardless of lead quality.

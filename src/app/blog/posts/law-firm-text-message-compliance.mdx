Text messages get opened within three minutes on average, and law firms have noticed. Intake teams now text leads before they finish reading the email confirmation. But **law firm text message compliance** has become one of the least understood areas of legal marketing, and the gap between "we text our leads" and "we text our leads legally" is where six-figure settlements live.

The Telephone Consumer Protection Act treats a text message the same way it treats a robocall. Add carrier-level registration rules that most marketing vendors never mention, and you have a channel firms are using constantly while understanding poorly.

## Why Law Firm Text Message Compliance Is Suddenly a Bigger Risk

TCPA litigation volume tied to text messaging has climbed steadily as plaintiffs' attorneys — many of whom built practices around this exact statute — shifted focus from calls to texts. Statutory damages run $500 per violation, tripling to $1,500 for willful violations, and a single mismanaged SMS campaign to a purchased list can generate thousands of individual texts in days.

Law firms are exposed twice over. They're a defendant class like any other business that texts consumers, and they're also the plaintiff's bar's favorite hunting ground because TCPA cases are contingency-friendly and firms are assumed to know better. A firm that gets sued for the same violations it markets to clients about is a story reporters and bar counsel both enjoy.

The second driver is newer: wireless carriers have tightened enforcement on **unregistered business texting**, filtering or blocking messages from numbers that haven't completed the required registration process. Firms that skipped this step aren't just risking a lawsuit — their texts increasingly never arrive at all.

Client communication preferences are shifting fast enough that firms feel pressure to keep pace regardless of the risk. The [Clio Legal Trends Report](https://www.clio.com/resources/legal-trends/) has tracked rising consumer expectations for fast, text-based responses year over year, which is exactly the dynamic pushing intake teams toward channels they haven't fully compliance-checked.

## The TCPA Text Message Rules Governing Attorney Outreach

The TCPA requires **prior express written consent** before sending marketing texts to a wireless number, and that consent has specific formal requirements — it must be in writing (electronic signatures count), it must clearly disclose that the consumer is agreeing to receive automated marketing messages, and it can't be buried in unrelated terms of service.

A lead who fills out a contact form asking for a callback has not consented to a text campaign. This is the single most common mistake firms make: treating any inbound lead as blanket permission to text, call, and email indefinitely. The consent has to match the channel and the purpose.

Key distinctions that matter in practice:

- **Transactional versus marketing texts** — a text confirming an appointment carries lower consent requirements than a text promoting a new practice area or asking for a referral.
- **Consent scope** — consent obtained for one campaign or one intake source doesn't automatically extend to a purchased or aggregated list.
- **Revocation** — consumers can revoke consent through any reasonable method, not just "STOP," and firms must honor it across every list the number appears on, not just the one that received the reply.

Firms that already have telemarketing call protocols in place often assume those cover texting too. They don't fully overlap — see our breakdown of [law firm do not call compliance](/blog/law-firm-do-not-call-compliance) for how the calling rules differ from the texting rules, and where they intersect under the same consent framework covered in our [TCPA compliance guide for law firms](/blog/tcpa-compliance-law-firms).

## 10DLC Registration: The Carrier Rule Most Firms Miss

Application-to-Person (A2P) 10DLC registration is a carrier-enforced system, not a federal statute, but it functions like a compliance requirement because unregistered traffic gets throttled or blocked outright by AT&T, Verizon, and T-Mobile. Every firm sending business texts from a standard 10-digit local number needs to register the brand and the specific campaign use case through their SMS platform.

The registration process asks firms to declare what they're texting about — appointment reminders, lead follow-up, marketing promotions — and carriers apply different throughput limits and filtering rules to each category. A firm that registers as "customer care" and then runs cold outreach campaigns risks having its entire number range flagged, which can silently kill delivery on every message, including the transactional ones clients are expecting.

Most legal intake platforms and CRMs handle 10DLC registration during onboarding, but plenty of firms never confirm it was completed correctly, or they add new campaign types later without updating the registration. If your open rates or delivery rates have quietly dropped, an unregistered or misclassified campaign is the first thing to check.

Enforcement in this area moves quickly, and coverage from outlets like the [National Law Review](https://www.natlawreview.com) is a reasonable way to track new TCPA guidance and carrier rule changes without relying solely on a vendor's word that a platform is "compliant."

## Building SMS Opt-In Compliance Into Your Intake Process

**SMS opt-in compliance** starts at the point of lead capture, not at the point of the first text. The consent language on your intake form, landing page, or paper sign-in sheet is the document that will get produced in discovery if a case ever gets filed.

A defensible opt-in flow includes:

1. A clear, unbundled checkbox or statement specific to text messaging — not folded into a general "I agree to be contacted" disclaimer.
2. Disclosure of message frequency, that message and data rates may apply, and how to opt out.
3. A timestamped record of consent tied to the specific number and the specific form or call where it was captured.
4. A double opt-in confirmation text for any list built from purchased or third-party lead sources, since consent captured by someone else is difficult to defend in court.

This matters even more when leads originate from a vendor rather than the firm's own website. Before texting anyone from a purchased or brokered list, confirm exactly how and where consent was captured — our guide to [lead generation rules for attorneys](/blog/lead-generation-rules-for-attorneys) covers the ethics obligations that apply on top of the TCPA consent requirement, and it's worth reading alongside any vendor's data documentation before the first message goes out.

## Bar Ethics Rules on Attorney Text Message Marketing

Beyond federal statute, state bar rules on solicitation apply to **attorney text message marketing** the same way they apply to direct mail or cold calls. Most states treat an unsolicited text to a potential client, particularly one who hasn't had prior professional contact with the firm, as a form of real-time or near-real-time solicitation subject to stricter rules than general advertising.

The [American Bar Association's Model Rule 7.3](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/professional_responsibility/publications/model_rules_of_professional_conduct/model_rules_of_professional_conduct_table_of_contents/) on solicitation of clients is the baseline most states adapt, and it draws a meaningful line between contacting someone who has already reached out to the firm versus initiating contact with a stranger. A text sent thirty seconds after a web form submission generally falls on the safer side of that line. A text sent to a name pulled from a public accident report or court filing does not.

Firms operating across multiple states need to check each jurisdiction's specific solicitation rules, since some states impose cooling-off periods for personal injury outreach specifically, which can conflict with the "text fast" instinct that otherwise drives conversion.

## Speed-to-Lead Texting Without Creating Liability

The tension every intake team feels is real: response speed is one of the strongest predictors of whether a lead signs, and texting is the fastest channel available. Our data on [speed to lead for law firms](/blog/speed-to-lead-law-firms) shows conversion probability dropping sharply after the first few minutes, which is exactly why firms lean so hard on automated texting.

The fix isn't slowing down — it's making sure consent capture happens at the same speed as the response. A well-built intake form that captures explicit SMS consent in the same submission that triggers the auto-response solves both problems at once: the lead gets contacted immediately, and the firm has a timestamped, form-specific consent record if anyone ever asks for one.

## A Law Firm Text Message Compliance Checklist

Before your intake team sends another marketing or follow-up text, confirm these items are actually in place rather than assumed:

- Written, unbundled SMS consent captured at the point of lead intake, with a timestamp and source record.
- A2P 10DLC brand and campaign registration completed and matched to the actual message types being sent.
- A documented, honored opt-out process that applies across every list a number appears on.
- Clear separation between transactional messages (appointment confirmations, case updates) and marketing messages (promotions, referral asks).
- State-specific solicitation rules reviewed for every jurisdiction where the firm actively markets.
- Vendor or purchased-list consent documentation reviewed before any number from that source is texted.

Firms that treat this as a one-time setup rather than an ongoing audit are the ones that end up explaining a compliance gap to a judge instead of a marketing director.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is texting leads without consent illegal for law firms?

Yes. Sending marketing text messages to a wireless number without prior express written consent violates the TCPA, exposing the firm to statutory damages of $500 to $1,500 per message. Transactional texts tied to an existing client relationship carry a lower consent bar, but marketing and prospecting texts require documented, specific consent.

### What is 10DLC and does my law firm need to register?

10DLC (10-digit long code) is the carrier system for registering business text traffic sent from standard local numbers. Any firm sending business SMS at volume — appointment reminders, lead follow-up, or marketing — needs to register its brand and campaign type, or carriers can throttle or block the messages entirely.

### Does TCPA consent for phone calls also cover text messages?

No. Consent is generally tied to the specific channel and purpose it was captured for, so a lead who agreed to receive a callback hasn't automatically consented to text messages. Firms need separate, clearly disclosed consent language for SMS, distinct from calling consent.

### How fast can a law firm text a new lead without violating solicitation rules?

Responding immediately to someone who has just submitted a contact form is generally treated differently than initiating contact with someone who hasn't reached out, since the former is a response rather than a solicitation. Firms should still confirm their specific state bar's solicitation rules, since several states impose waiting periods for personal injury outreach.

### What should a law firm do if a lead replies STOP to a text?

Honor the opt-out immediately and suppress that number across every campaign and list it appears on, not just the one that received the reply. The TCPA recognizes reasonable opt-out methods beyond the word "STOP," so any clear request to stop texting should be treated the same way.

The most important document in a car crash file used to be the at-fault driver's declarations page. Increasingly, it is your own client's. **Uninsured motorist claims** have moved from an occasional fallback to a core part of auto practice, because a growing share of the drivers causing crashes have no coverage, or nowhere near enough.

The Insurance Research Council estimated that 15.4% of U.S. drivers were uninsured in 2023, and that roughly one in three were either uninsured or underinsured. In practical terms, a serious injury case has about a one-in-three chance of running out of defendant money before it runs out of damages.

That changes what a "good" auto lead looks like. Clear liability and real injuries are no longer enough to predict a fee.

## Why Uninsured Motorist Claims Are Growing

Three forces are pushing in the same direction, and none of them is likely to reverse soon.

**Premiums climbed faster than household budgets.** The motor vehicle insurance component of the Consumer Price Index rose by roughly 20% year over year in early 2024, one of the steepest runs in decades. When premiums spike, drivers at the margin drop coverage entirely or strip it down to the legal minimum.

**The rate varies enormously by state.** National averages hide the real exposure. In states such as Mississippi and New Mexico, IRC estimates put the uninsured share above one in five drivers, while several northeastern states sit in the single digits.

**Medical costs outran minimum limits.** A single emergency department visit with imaging and a short admission can consume a $25,000 policy before an orthopedic surgeon is ever involved. Drivers carrying minimum limits are technically insured and practically judgment-proof.

The third point is the one firms underestimate. The underinsured driver is now a bigger problem for **personal injury case value** than the fully uninsured one, simply because there are more of them.

## The Minimum-Limits Problem

Several states have recognized that their statutory minimums were set for a different era. The changes are real, but they are modest.

- **California** raised its minimums to $30,000 per person and $60,000 per accident for bodily injury on January 1, 2025, the first increase since 1967.
- **Virginia** moved to $50,000/$100,000 for bodily injury on January 1, 2025.
- **North Carolina** increased its minimums to $50,000/$100,000 effective July 1, 2025.
- **Florida** still does not require most drivers to carry bodily injury liability coverage at all.

Higher minimums help at the low end of the docket. They do little for a client with a spinal fusion and $400,000 in damages.

There is also a second-order effect. Raising mandatory **policy limits** raises premiums, which pushes some drivers out of the insured pool altogether. A state can lift its minimums and see its uninsured rate rise at the same time.

Rideshare coverage is moving the other way in at least one major market. California legislation taking effect in 2026 sharply reduced the uninsured and underinsured coverage that rideshare companies must carry for passengers, from $1 million down to $60,000 per person. Firms that built a niche on rideshare passenger cases should rerun their numbers.

## How UM/UIM Claims Change the Case

A liability claim and a first-party claim against the client's own carrier look similar in the demand letter and very different everywhere else. **UM/UIM claims** are contract claims, and the contract controls.

### The policy sets the rules

Deadlines, notice requirements, and dispute procedures come from the policy language and state insurance statutes, not only from the tort statute of limitations. Some policies require prompt written notice of a potential claim. Some states allow or require arbitration instead of a jury trial.

Missing a contractual condition can sink a claim that was otherwise worth six figures.

### Settlement with the tortfeasor is a trap

Most **underinsured motorist coverage** contains a consent-to-settle provision protecting the carrier's subrogation rights. Accept the at-fault driver's full coverage and sign a release without the UIM carrier's consent, and the client may forfeit the larger recovery.

Every state handles this differently. Your settlement workflow needs a hard stop that forces a UIM check before any release is signed.

### Stacking and offsets decide the number

Whether a client can stack coverage across multiple vehicles or policies varies by state and by policy form. So does the question of whether UIM coverage sits on top of the liability recovery or is reduced by it.

The difference is not academic. A client with $100,000 in UIM coverage and a $50,000 tortfeasor settlement may have $150,000 available in an add-on state and $100,000 in a reduction state.

### Your client's insurer is now the adversary

The carrier that sold the policy owes duties of good faith, and it also evaluates the claim with the same tools it uses against third parties. Expect the software-driven valuation described in our analysis of [insurance claims algorithms](/blog/insurance-claims-algorithms-personal-injury), applied to a policyholder who assumed their own company would be on their side.

That gap between expectation and reality is where client communication matters most.

## What Uninsured Motorist Claims Mean for Intake and Screening

If coverage determines recovery, coverage has to be captured at the first conversation rather than discovered at month four. Most intake scripts still ask about the other driver's insurance and stop there.

Add these questions to every auto intake:

1. **Does the caller have their own auto policy, and with which carrier?** Ask for the declarations page by text or email before the consultation.
2. **Does anyone in the household own a vehicle or carry auto insurance?** Resident-relative provisions frequently extend UM coverage to family members who were not driving, or were on foot or on a bicycle.
3. **Was the caller working, or in a vehicle owned by someone else?** Employer and vehicle-owner policies may apply before the caller's own.
4. **Did the caller ever sign a form rejecting or reducing UM coverage?** Many states require a specific written rejection, and a defective waiver can restore coverage the client believes they declined.
5. **Is there an umbrella policy?** Some umbrellas include excess UM/UIM coverage and some exclude it.
6. **Did the other vehicle stop?** Hit-and-run claims often carry strict reporting deadlines and, in some states, physical contact requirements.

Building these into a structured [law firm intake script](/blog/law-firm-intake-script) takes about ninety seconds per call. It turns an unknown into a number.

The same data should feed your decline decisions. Disciplined [law firm case screening](/blog/law-firm-case-screening) means separating the serious injury with $250,000 in stacked UIM from the serious injury with a minimum-limits defendant and no first-party coverage at all.

Both callers sound identical in the first two minutes.

## The Revenue Impact for Auto Practices

The growth in uninsured motorist claims reshapes firm economics in ways that do not show up in lead counts.

**Recoverable value compresses.** When a third of at-fault drivers are uninsured or underinsured, average fees per signed auto case fall unless the firm is systematically finding first-party coverage. Firms that track fee per case by coverage source usually discover that UIM recoveries are carrying more of the docket than anyone realized.

**Some cases get faster, some get slower.** A policy-limits tender on a clear-liability case can close in weeks. The UIM claim that follows can add months of documentation, examinations under oath, and arbitration scheduling, which matters if you are managing case cycle time as a growth lever.

**Practice mix shifts the exposure.** Motorcyclists, pedestrians, and cyclists suffer severe injuries at low speeds and are frequently hit by minimally insured drivers. If you are investing in [motorcycle accident lead generation](/blog/motorcycle-accident-lead-generation), your recovery depends heavily on how many riders bought their own UM coverage.

**Bad faith becomes a real practice line.** First-party **insurance bad faith** exposure exists in many states when a carrier unreasonably delays or denies a valid claim. For firms equipped to litigate it, a stonewalled UIM file can become more valuable than the underlying injury claim.

The Clio [Legal Trends Report](https://www.clio.com/resources/legal-trends/) has shown for years that firms measuring performance at the matter level outperform those that do not. Coverage source is one of the most useful fields a plaintiff firm can add to that measurement.

## A Coverage-First Workflow

Firms handling uninsured motorist claims well tend to follow the same sequence on every auto file:

- **Day 1:** Collect the client's declarations page and identify every household policy.
- **Week 1:** Send written notice of a potential UM/UIM claim to every carrier that might owe coverage, even before liability limits are known.
- **Week 2:** Request the at-fault driver's limits through the disclosure mechanism your state allows.
- **Before any release:** Confirm consent-to-settle requirements in writing with the UIM carrier.
- **At closing:** Record the recovery by source so the data informs future screening and marketing decisions.

None of this requires new technology. It requires treating coverage investigation as an intake task rather than a litigation task.

For doctrinal depth, the ABA's [Tort Trial and Insurance Practice Section](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/tort_trial_insurance_practice/) publishes state-by-state insurance coverage materials, and the [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/) tracks appellate decisions on stacking, offsets, and bad faith as they are issued.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are uninsured motorist claims?

Uninsured motorist claims are first-party claims an injured person makes against their own auto insurer when the at-fault driver has no liability coverage or cannot be identified. The insurer steps into the shoes of the uninsured driver and pays damages up to the policy's UM limits. Because the claim arises from the insurance contract, the policy language and state insurance law control deadlines and procedures.

### What is the difference between uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage?

Uninsured coverage applies when the at-fault driver has no insurance, while underinsured motorist coverage applies when that driver's limits are too low to cover the damages. In an underinsured claim, the client typically recovers the at-fault driver's limits first and then pursues their own carrier for the shortfall. State law determines whether the two amounts are added together or offset against each other.

### How do policy limits affect personal injury case value?

Policy limits set the practical ceiling on what most auto cases recover, regardless of how severe the injuries are. A case with $500,000 in damages against a driver carrying $30,000 in coverage is worth roughly $30,000 unless additional coverage exists. That is why identifying every available policy, including the client's own, is central to valuing a case accurately.

### Can a client settle with the at-fault driver before making a UM/UIM claim?

A client can usually settle with the at-fault driver first, but only after satisfying the UIM carrier's consent-to-settle requirements. Signing a release without that consent can waive the underinsured claim in many states because it destroys the carrier's subrogation rights. Firms should confirm consent in writing before any release is executed.

### Do uninsured motorist claims take longer to resolve than standard auto cases?

Uninsured motorist claims often take longer because they add a second claim process on top of the liability claim. The carrier may require recorded statements, medical examinations, or arbitration under the policy terms. Early written notice and complete documentation are the most reliable ways to shorten the timeline.
